DC Networks Lab

Electrical Engineering I · Module 92315 · Chapter 3

Predict how a network shares its voltages and currents before the charge starts to move. Rate your confidence, watch the result appear, then build your own circuits.

How the lab works

  1. Predict. Commit to an answer before the stage changes.
  2. Rate your confidence. A sure but wrong answer marks an idea to revisit first.
  3. Reveal. Watch the charge flow and the values appear, and compare them with your prediction.
  4. Explore. Press Explore freely at any time, or finish the four challenges, to set up your own circuit.

3.2 Real sources: the terminal line

A real source is an ideal one with an internal resistance. Every load picks one point on the same straight line between the open-circuit voltage and the short-circuit current; watch the operating point move as the load changes.

3.4 Kirchhoff’s laws: a node and a mesh

At a node the currents balance; round a mesh the voltages add to zero. Each arrow only says how to count the sign, so a value against its arrow simply comes out negative.

3.5 Dividers: what a load does to the tap

A series chain splits a voltage like its resistances, as long as nothing draws current from the tap. Connect a load and it sits in parallel with the lower resistor, so the tap voltage sags.

3.5 Maximum power: matching the load

The load power is zero for a short circuit and for an open circuit, and peaks in between. The efficiency keeps rising with the load, so the best load for power is not the best for efficiency.
