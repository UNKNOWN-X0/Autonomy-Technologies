Appendix A: Trigonometric identities

A.1 Reciprocal and quotient identities

These definitions connect sine, cosine and tangent to their reciprocals. Quotient identities express tangent and cotangent using sine and cosine.

Reciprocalssin u = 1csc u   cos u = 1sec u   tan u = 1cot ucsc u = 1sin u   sec u = 1cos u   cot u = 1tan u
Quotientstan u = sin ucos u     cot u = cos usin uThe expressions require nonzero denominators: for example, tan u is undefined when cos u = 0.

A.2 Pythagorean identities

Start with the unit-circle equation sin² u + cos² u = 1. Dividing by cos² u or sin² u gives the other two forms.

Three equivalent formssin2 u + cos2 u = 11 + tan2 u = sec2 u1 + cot2 u = csc2 u

A.3 Cofunction and even–odd identities

Complementary angles exchange each trigonometric function with its cofunction. Changing the sign of an angle leaves cosine and secant unchanged; sine, tangent, cosecant and cotangent change sign.

Cofunctionssin(π2 − u) = cos u    cos(π2 − u) = sin utan(π2 − u) = cot u    cot(π2 − u) = tan usec(π2 − u) = csc u    csc(π2 − u) = sec u
Even and oddsin(−u) = −sin u   cos(−u) = cos u   tan(−u) = −tan ucsc(−u) = −csc u   sec(−u) = sec u   cot(−u) = −cot u

A.4 Sum and difference formulas

These expand a function of a combined angle into products of functions of the two separate angles. The cosine formula changes the sign between its two terms.

Sine and cosinesin(u + v) = sin u cos v + cos u sin vsin(u − v) = sin u cos v − cos u sin vcos(u + v) = cos u cos v − sin u sin vcos(u − v) = cos u cos v + sin u sin v
Tangenttan(u ± v) = tan u ± tan v1 ∓ tan u tan vUse matching signs: the upper signs give the sum formula, and the lower signs give the difference formula. The denominator must be nonzero.

A.5 Double-angle and triple-angle formulas

Put v = u in the sum formulas to get double angles. The three triple-angle formulas follow by applying them again and simplifying.

Double anglessin 2u = 2 sin u cos ucos 2u = cos2 u − sin2 u = 2 cos2 u − 1 = 1 − 2 sin2 utan 2u = 2 tan u1 − tan2 u
Triple anglessin 3u = 3 sin u − 4 sin3 ucos 3u = 4 cos3 u − 3 cos utan 3u = 3 tan u − tan3 u1 − 3 tan2 u

A.6 Power reduction and half angles

Power-reduction identities replace squares of sine or cosine with a double-angle cosine. Half-angle identities use the same cosine and a sign chosen from the quadrant of u2.

Power reductionsin2 u = 1 − cos 2u2    cos2 u = 1 + cos 2u2tan2 u = 1 − cos 2u1 + cos 2u
Half anglessin(u2) = ±√1 − cos u2    cos(u2) = ±√1 + cos u2tan(u2) = 1 − cos usin u = sin u1 + cos uThe ± sign is determined by whether sin or cos is positive or negative at the half-angle. The quotient forms for tan(u2) require their denominators to be nonzero.

A.7 Product-to-sum formulas

These convert products into sums or differences. They are useful when integrating products or comparing expressions with different frequencies.

Products of sine and cosinesin u sin v = ½[cos(u − v) − cos(u + v)]cos u cos v = ½[cos(u − v) + cos(u + v)]sin u cos v = ½[sin(u + v) + sin(u − v)]cos u sin v = ½[sin(u + v) − sin(u − v)]

A.8 Sum-to-product formulas

These reverse the previous transformations, writing a sum or difference as a product. They can turn pairs of sine or cosine terms into one compact expression.

Sums and differencessin u + sin v = 2 sin(u + v2) cos(u − v2)sin u − sin v = 2 cos(u + v2) sin(u − v2)cos u + cos v = 2 cos(u + v2) cos(u − v2)cos u − cos v = −2 sin(u + v2) sin(u − v2)
