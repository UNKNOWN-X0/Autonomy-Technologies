Appendix B: Calculation rules for sums and products

Mathematics for Engineers I · Module 62765 · Personal reference

Personal study supplement supplied by the student; not a lecturer handout or official course material. Use these rules to rewrite, split and evaluate finite sums and products. They accompany Chapter 2.4, Sums, Products, Binomial Coefficient. Throughout, m ≤ n are integers.

B.1 Splitting and reordering

A finite sum or product may be cut into pieces, read backwards, or pulled apart term by term. The result does not change, because addition and multiplication are associative and commutative.

Sums∑k=mnak = am + ∑k=m+1nak∑k=1nak = ∑k=1nan+1−k∑k=mn(ak + bk) = ∑k=mnak + ∑k=mnbkSplitting off the first term needs at least one term, m ≤ n. Reversing pairs the index k with n + 1 − k, so k = 1 picks an and k = n picks a1.
Products∏k=mnak = am · ∏k=m+1nak∏k=1nak = ∏k=1nan+1−k∏k=mn(ak · bk) = ∏k=mnak · ∏k=mnbkEach product rule is the sum rule with ∑ replaced by ∏ and + replaced by ·. There is no product rule for a sum of factors: ∏(ak + bk) does not separate.

B.2 Double sums and index shifts

With finitely many terms, the order of two summations can be swapped. An index shift renames the running index and moves both limits the opposite way, so the same terms are added.

Swapping the order∑l=rs∑k=mnalk = ∑k=mn∑l=rsalk∏l=rs∏k=mnalk = ∏k=mn∏l=rsalkBoth limits must be fixed numbers. When the inner limits depend on the outer index, for example k ≤ l, rewrite the region before swapping.
Shifting the index∑k=1nak = ∑l=2n+1al−1∑k=mnak = ∑l=m+pn+pal−pSubstitute l = k + p: raise both limits by p and lower the subscript by p. The same shift works for ∏.

B.3 Constant terms and telescoping

A constant inside the operator is repeated once for each index value, and k = m to n gives n − m + 1 values. In a telescoping sum or product, neighbouring terms cancel and only the two ends remain.

Constant summands and factors∑k=mna = (n − m + 1) a∏k=mna = an − m + 1Valid for m ≤ n. Example: ∑k=374 = 5 · 4 = 20, and ∏k=374 = 45.
Telescoping∑k=mn(ak − ak+1) = am − an+1∏k=mnakak+1 = aman+1The last term left over is an+1, not an: write out k = m, m + 1 and watch −am+1 cancel +am+1. The product form needs every ak ≠ 0 for m + 1 ≤ k ≤ n + 1.

B.4 Constant factors

This is the one place where sums and products behave differently. A sum adds the factor once per term, so it comes out once; a product multiplies it once per term, so it comes out as a power.

Factor out of a sum∑k=mncak = c∑k=mnak∑k=152k = 2∑k=15k = 2 · 15 = 30
Factor out of a product∏k=mncak = cn − m + 1∏k=mnak∏k=152k = 25∏k=15k = 32 · 120 = 3840Pulling out only one factor c is the classic mistake: it gives 2 · 120 = 240 instead of 3840.
