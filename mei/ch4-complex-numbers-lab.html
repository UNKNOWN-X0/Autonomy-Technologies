Complex Numbers Lab

Mathematics for Engineers I · Module 62765 · Chapter 4

Predict where a complex number lands in the plane before it moves. Rate your confidence, watch the arrows turn and stretch, then explore your own numbers.

How the lab works

  1. Predict. Commit to an answer before the stage changes.
  2. Rate your confidence. A sure but wrong answer marks an idea to revisit first.
  3. Reveal. Watch the result appear in the Gaussian plane and compare it with your prediction.
  4. Explore. Press Explore freely at any time, or finish the four challenges, to try your own numbers.

4.1 Arithmetic in the plane: sums, products and quotients

Adding works like arrows placed end to end. Multiplying turns and stretches: watch where the product lands and how far it is from the origin.

4.1 Polar form: length, angle and the conjugate

The same point can be given by its parts a and b or by its length |z| and angle φ. The quadrant decides which angle arctan really means.

4.2 Powers: de Moivre’s spiral

Each further power multiplies the length by |z| and adds the angle once more. Lengths above 1 spiral outwards, below 1 inwards, and exactly 1 stays on the circle.

4.2 Roots: corners of a regular polygon

Taking an n-th root divides the angle by n, and every full turn added first gives another root. The n roots share one length and are spread evenly round a circle.

After the challenges, choose a number and how many roots below.

4.3 Complex functions: zeros, exponential and logarithm

Real coefficients mirror zeros in the real axis. The exponential stretches by eˣ and turns by y, so adding a full turn to y changes nothing, which is why the logarithm must choose one angle.
